# MTG Archetype Showcase

A set of 60-card kitchen-table decks, one per archetype, built to show what
each lane of Magic feels like to play. Use this file to pick the next deck to
build and to track the ones that are done.

## Ground rules

- 60-card main deck, 4-copy limit, no sideboard required.
- Format legality does not matter. Every card is proxied, so price does not
  matter.
- Each card must illustrate its archetype. A strong card that does not teach
  the deck's plan does not make the cut.
- All decks share one power band, so a game between any two shows the
  archetypes, not a power gap.
- Power band: Modern-level card quality. A few Legacy-only cards are fine when
  they are no stronger than Modern staples. No fast mana (Moxen, Sol Ring),
  no free counterspells, and no turn-1 kills.
- No exclusions: infinite combos, stax, extra turns, and land destruction are
  all allowed.

## The four pillars

Almost every archetype is a flavor of one of four strategies, or a blend of
two.

- **Aggro** wins fast with cheap threats before the opponent sets up.
- **Midrange** trades one-for-one, then wins with better-quality cards.
- **Control** answers everything, then wins late with a few threats.
- **Combo** ignores the opponent and assembles a specific winning set of
  cards.

The classic triangle: aggro beats combo (too fast), combo beats control (too
many threats at once, or too resilient), control beats aggro (answers and
sweepers), and midrange sits in the middle. Tempo and ramp are hybrids:
tempo is aggro plus control, ramp is midrange that skips ahead on mana.

## Archetypes

### Aggro

#### A1. Burn

Cheap red spells that hit the opponent directly, backed by a few hasty
creatures. Counts the opponent down from 20 as fast as possible.
Colors: R. Try it if you like math, speed, and short games.

#### A2. Go-wide tokens

Many small creatures or token makers, then a card that pumps the whole team
for a big alpha strike. Colors: W, often G or R. Try it if you like a board
full of creatures.

#### A3. Tribal lords

All one creature type (Goblins, Elves, Merfolk, Zombies) with "lords" that
buff every member. Each creature makes the others better. Colors: depends on
tribe. Try it if you like flavor and synergy.

#### A4. Prowess and spells-matter aggro

Creatures that grow or trigger whenever you cast a noncreature spell, fueled
by cheap cantrips and pump spells. Colors: UR. Try it if you like chaining
cheap spells in one turn.

#### A5. Infect and poison

Creatures that deal damage as poison counters; 10 poison wins. Pump spells
turn one unblocked attacker into a lethal hit. Colors: G, often UG or BG.
Try it if you like an alternate path to victory.

#### A6. Artifact aggro

Floods the board with cheap or free artifacts, then uses creatures that scale
with artifact count. Colors: mostly colorless, light U or R. Try it if you
like explosive starts.

#### A7. Voltron (auras and equipment)

One hard-to-kill creature loaded with auras or equipment until it wins in a
few swings. Colors: W, G, or GW. Try it if you like building one big hero.

#### A8. Hatebears (Death and Taxes)

Efficient small creatures whose abilities tax or disrupt the opponent, so you
attack while they struggle to act. Colors: W. Try it if you like being an
annoying aggro deck.

### Tempo

#### T1. Flash tempo

A few cheap evasive threats, protected by counterspells and bounce. Spends
mana on the opponent's turn and stays one step ahead. Colors: U, often UR or
UB. Try it if you like reading the opponent and holding up tricks.

#### T2. Ninjas and evasion

Cheap unblockable creatures that swap into bigger threats once unblocked,
drawing cards with each hit. Colors: U, often UB. Try it if you like sneaky
combat tricks.

### Midrange

#### M1. Rock (good-stuff)

Removal, discard, and efficient threats. No single synergy; every card is a
good trade. Colors: BG, often BGx. Try it if you like flexible,
decision-heavy games.

#### M2. Aristocrats

Makes cheap creatures, sacrifices them for value, and drains the opponent each
time something dies. Colors: B, often BW or BR. Try it if you like engines
and death triggers.

#### M3. Blink and ETB value

Creatures with "enters the battlefield" effects, plus cards that exile and
return them to repeat those effects. Colors: W, often WU. Try it if you like
grinding out value.

#### M4. +1/+1 counters

Creatures that place, double, or move +1/+1 counters, so the board grows a
little every turn. Colors: G, often GW. Try it if you like a growing board.

#### M5. Lifegain

Gains life constantly and uses payoffs that grow creatures or drain the
opponent when life is gained. Colors: W, often WB. Try it if you like being
hard to kill.

#### M6. Enchantress

Enchantments that draw a card whenever you cast an enchantment, building into
an overwhelming board. Colors: GW. Try it if you like card draw engines.

#### M7. Graveyard value

Fills its own graveyard, then recasts or returns creatures and spells from
there. Colors: BG. Try it if you like using every resource twice.

#### M8. Toolbox

Many one-of answers and threats, with tutors that find the right one for the
situation. Colors: G, often GW or GB. Try it if you like puzzle solving.

### Control

#### C1. Draw-go

Mostly instants: counterspells, card draw, and removal. Passes the turn with
mana open and wins late with a few finishers. Colors: U, often UW. Try it if
you like saying no.

#### C2. Tap-out control

Board sweepers, spot removal, and expensive card-advantage spells, then a
large finisher. Colors: W, B, or WB. Try it if you like resetting the board.

#### C3. Superfriends

Planeswalkers backed by removal and sweepers. The walkers take over a board
that is kept clear. Colors: multicolor. Try it if you like planeswalkers.

#### C4. Prison and stax

Permanents that lock the opponent out: tax spells, stop untaps, or restrict
attacks. Wins slowly once the lock is in place. Colors: W or colorless. Try it
if you like breaking rules.

#### C5. Mill

Puts the opponent's library into their graveyard; they lose when they can't
draw. Colors: U, often UB. Try it if you like an unusual win condition.

#### C6. Turbo fog and pillowfort

Prevents combat damage or makes attacking too costly, while a slow win
condition takes over. Colors: GW or WU. Try it if you like frustrating
attackers.

#### C7. Group slug

Punishes everyone each turn (damage, life loss, discard) and survives the
pain longer than the opponent. Colors: R or B. Try it if you like a race in
which both players lose.

### Ramp

#### R1. Big mana

Ramps early with mana creatures and land search, then casts huge threats
years ahead of schedule. Colors: G, sometimes colorless. Try it if you like
casting giant monsters.

#### R2. Lands matter

Plays extra lands each turn and triggers landfall payoffs or lands that
sacrifice for value. Colors: G, often GR. Try it if you like a different kind
of engine.

### Combo

#### K1. Storm

Chains many cheap spells and rituals in one turn, then casts a finisher that
counts the spells. Colors: R or UR. Try it if you like one big, careful turn.

#### K2. Reanimator

Discards or mills a giant creature early, then returns it from the graveyard
on turn two or three. Colors: B, often UB. Try it if you like cheating the
mana system.

#### K3. Cheat into play

Puts a huge creature onto the battlefield without casting it, through effects
that skip mana cost. Colors: R, U, or G. Try it if you like the moment a
giant arrives early.

#### K4. Two-card infinite

Two cards that combine for unlimited damage, tokens, or mana. The rest of the
deck finds the pieces and protects them. Colors: varies. Try it if you like
"I win" moments.

#### K5. Dredge

Uses the graveyard as its hand: replaces draws with mills, then gets free
creatures and effects back from the graveyard. Colors: B, often BR. Try it if
you like a deck that plays itself backward.

#### K6. Alternate win conditions

Cards that say "you win the game" under a specific condition; the deck builds
toward that condition. Colors: varies. Try it if you like unique goals.

#### K7. Extra turns

Chains extra-turn spells with card draw and recursion, so the opponent never
gets another turn. Colors: U. Try it if you like a long solo puzzle turn.

## Suggested first round

Start with one deck per pillar so you can each try a different lane and see
the triangle play out:

1. A1. Burn (aggro)
1. M1. Rock (midrange)
1. C1. Draw-go (control)
1. K2. Reanimator (combo)
1. R1. Big mana (ramp)
